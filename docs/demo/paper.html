Detecting Robot Collisions with a Generalized-Momentum Observer: A Short Tutorial

Abstract—Robots that share space with people must notice unexpected contact quickly. This tutorial derives a residual signal from the generalized momentum of a manipulator. The residual needs only joint positions, joint velocities and commanded motor torques, avoids numerical differentiation of velocity, and behaves as a first-order filtered estimate of the external joint torque. We discuss threshold selection, the effect of friction, and how the same signal supports isolating the link in contact.

I. Introduction

Collision handling is usually split into detection, isolation and identification. Detection decides whether a contact occurred at all; isolation finds which link was touched; identification estimates the direction and magnitude of the contact force. All three benefit from a signal that is zero in free motion and that reacts immediately when an external torque appears.

A direct approach would invert the dynamic model and compare the commanded torque with the torque needed for the measured motion. That requires joint accelerations, which are noisy when obtained by differentiating encoder velocity. The observer presented here avoids this problem by working with momentum instead of acceleration.

Because a contact on link k cannot produce torque at joints beyond k, the last joint whose residual is significantly nonzero indicates the earliest link that may be in contact. This simple rule isolates the collided link without any additional sensors.

V. Conclusion

The momentum residual offers a practical collision detector: it uses only proprioceptive signals, needs no acceleration estimate, and provides a filtered estimate of the external torque that also supports isolation. Its main limitation is sensitivity to model and friction errors, which sets a lower bound on usable thresholds.
